SULIVER

Sustainable Livelihoods & Economic Resilience

Global Sustainable Development Report (GSDR 2019) – Technical Policy Contribution

Expert input on sustainable development systems, policy integration, and global governance frameworks

1. Executive Summary

Dr. Chijioke J. Evoh contributed expert policy input to the United Nations Global Sustainable Development Report (2019) through an open call for submissions. The contribution supported global analysis on sustainable development systems, policy integration, and governance frameworks shaping development outcomes across diverse national contexts.

This engagement reflects participation in a multilateral knowledge process addressing sustainable development, policy coherence, and global development systems, contributing to evidence-based policy discourse at the United Nations level.

2. Methodology & Analytical Framework

Contribution provided through a structured open call for expert inputs, incorporating:

  • Development policy analysis and systems thinking approaches
  • Cross-sector perspectives on sustainable development and governance systems
  • Comparative insights from multi-country policy and institutional experience

The submission contributed to broader analytical synthesis undertaken within the Global Sustainable Development Report framework.

3. Key Insights & Major Findings

1.Contributed perspectives on policy integration and development systems alignment

2.Highlighted linkages between institutional governance, sustainability, and development outcomes

3.Supported global discourse on multi-sector policy coordination and implementation

4.Reinforced the importance of evidence-based policy frameworks in sustainable development

Dr. Chijioke J. Evoh is an Employment Policy Expert, Labour Market Policy Specialist, and Global Development Policy Advisor with over 15 years of international experience across employment policy, labour market systems, sustainable livelihoods, and Decent Work programming.
He has advised the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP), the International Labour Organization (ILO), and the United Nations Department of Economic and Social Affairs (UN DESA) on labour market reforms, employment systems, and inclusive growth strategies across multiple countries.
